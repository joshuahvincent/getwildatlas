Behind the facts

Where our animal facts come from

Every animal in Wild Atlas starts with named, public sources, not with an AI’s memory. Here are the organisations we draw on, and what we use each one for. And a real person reviews every image and every piece of text in the app before it goes out.

Last reviewed: October 16, 2026

The sources

  • IUCN Red List of Threatened SpeciesConservation

    How at-risk each animal is, its population trend, and the year it was assessed.

  • San Diego Zoo Wildlife AllianceZoo

    Scientific names, size, lifespan, diet, habitat, range and how animals live together.

  • Smithsonian InstitutionMuseum

    Diet, range and scientific names, especially for ocean animals.

  • Cornell Lab of Ornithology, All About BirdsBirds

    Diet, habitat and range for our feathered friends.

  • AmphibiaWebAmphibians

    Diet and habitat for frogs, toads and salamanders.

  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife ServiceWildlife agency

    Range and legal protection status for North American species.

  • CITES Species+ (UNEP-WCMC)Trade protection

    Which animals are protected from international trade.

  • American Kennel ClubDogs

    Dog breeds: size, lifespan, energy and how they behave with people and other dogs.

  • Cat Fanciers’ AssociationCats

    Cat breeds: size, lifespan, energy and how they behave.

  • GBIF, the Global Biodiversity Information FacilityClassification

    The scientific family tree, so every animal means the same species in every language.

  • WikidataIdentifiers

    A stable ID for each species, so the right animal is always matched to the right facts.

  • WikipediaCross-reference

    Used to confirm we have the right species and as a starting point for reading more. It is never the final word on a fact.

About the made-up animals. Our Mythical Menagerie pack features creatures from folklore and legend. They aren’t science facts, and we don’t present them as science.

How we check it

A real person reviews every image and every piece of text in Wild Atlas before it goes out. Every picture. Every word the app shows or says. Nothing is published because an AI said it was fine.

We use AI tools to help us find the data, then turn it into child-friendly language and writing. Alongside our human reviewers, we have two AI agents we’ve trained for specific jobs.

Dr. Attenborough checks biological accuracy. He’s named in tribute to our favorite naturalist, Sir David Attenborough.

Dr. Witt checks anything to do with kids. He works from a written rulebook: we don’t claim anything about how children learn or grow unless it can be backed up or carefully hedged, and every claim is matched to the right age group, younger explorers (about 2 to 5) or older explorers (about 6 to 10).

They are our additional eyes. They do the first pass, before a person looks at anything, and they flag issues as they find them for a human to check. Every image and every piece of text we create goes through them. There is never a time when they don’t look at the content before it goes out.

Found something that looks wrong? Tell us. Use the feedback button in the app, or email info@wildatlasapp.com. We’ll check it against the source and fix it.
