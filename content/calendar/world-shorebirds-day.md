  whatItCelebrates: |
    Shorebirds, also called waders, are birds that live and feed along beaches, mudflats and wetlands. Look for birds with long legs, long beaks and fast little feet.

    This day invites people everywhere to go out, look at them and count them.
  whyItMatters: |
    Many shorebirds travel very far each year and need safe places to rest and snack along the way. When people count shorebirds, they help scientists learn where the birds are. Families can help by giving beach birds some space.
  funFacts:
    - "A bar-tailed godwit once flew more than 11,000 kilometres (about 7,000 miles) without stopping, from Alaska to New Zealand."
    - "Sanderlings scurry back and forth at the edge of the waves, chasing tiny snacks as the water slides in and out."
    - "Shorebirds use their different beaks like tools. Some probe in the mud, and others pick from the surface."
    - "Plover chicks can run around soon after hatching."
    - "Red knots make some of the longest journeys of any bird."
  joinIn: |
    Visit a beach, lake shore or muddy riverbank near you, and see whether you can spot any shorebirds. Sit quietly, use your eyes or binoculars, and keep a good distance so you do not scare them. Count how many you see and write it down. You can join the Global Shorebird Count for free from wherever you are. Check the organisers' website for how it works, and ask a grown-up to help.
