Hummingbirds are so small, you might mistake one for a bumblebee!

{% figure "/assets/animals/hummingbird/cover.jpg", "A green and red hummingbird hovering beside a red flower in a colorful garden" %}

Their wings flap so fast that they make a humming sound. That's where the name comes from! Some hummingbirds flap about 80 times every second. Too fast to see!

And here's a wow: hummingbirds can hover. They hang in the air like a tiny helicopter. They can even fly backwards, which almost no other bird can do.

Why so busy? Hummingbirds sip sweet nectar from flowers with a long tongue that flicks in and out. Then they snack on tiny bugs, too. A hummingbird visits hundreds of flowers a day. It even remembers which ones it has visited!

Can you flap your arms as fast as you can? Now imagine doing it all day!

{% figure "/assets/animals/hummingbird/scale.jpg", "A tiny hummingbird shown zoomed in, next to a smiling 5-year-old explorer for scale" %}

A hummingbird is tiny. Many are shorter than your hand. And they weigh next to nothing: about 4 grams (0.14 ounces), less than a coin. It would take around 4,600 hummingbirds to weigh as much as one 5-year-old!

{% figure "/assets/animals/hummingbird/weight.jpg", "A scale with thousands of tiny hummingbirds on one side balancing a 5-year-old on the other" %}

Can you picture that many tiny birds on one big scale?

Here's one more wow. All that zooming takes lots of energy. So at night, a hummingbird goes into a deep, sleepy rest called torpor. Its heart slows way down so it can save its energy until morning.

A baby hummingbird hatches in a tiny nest, smaller than a teacup. Its mother builds it from soft plant fluff and sticky spider silk. She feeds her baby until it's ready to fly.

{% figure "/assets/animals/hummingbird/baby.jpg", "A baby hummingbird peeking out of a small nest on a flowering branch" %}

Can you whisper like a tiny nest? Shh. Someone is sleeping.
