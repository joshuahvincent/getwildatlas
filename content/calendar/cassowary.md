    The southern cassowary is listed as Least Concern worldwide on the IUCN Red List, but the picture is more mixed than that sounds. In Australia it is listed as Endangered, mainly because rainforest has been cleared and split up, and because cassowaries are sometimes hurt on roads. There's a hopeful story in the day itself: it was started in 2014 by conservationists in Far North Queensland, and rainforest replanting and community care for cassowary country are part of that work. A cassowary wears a helmet that grew on its own!

{% figure "/assets/animals/cassowary/cover.jpg", "A cassowary with black feathers, a bright blue neck and a tall helmet walking between mossy trees in a rainforest" %}

It's a huge bird that lives deep in the rainforest. Its neck and head are bright blue, with two red flaps that wobble when it walks. And on top of its head sits a tall, bony helmet called a casque. It may help the cassowary push through thick plants.

A cassowary can't fly at all. But its strong legs can make it run fast and jump high!

Can you stomp through the forest on big bird legs?

{% figure "/assets/animals/cassowary/scale.jpg", "A cassowary standing next to a 5-year-old explorer with a tall arrow showing the cassowary is about 1.6 meters, or 5 feet 3 inches, tall" %}

A big cassowary can stand about 1.6 meters (5 feet 3 inches) tall. That's about one and a half times as tall as you are, if you're five! It's one of the heaviest birds in the whole world.

{% figure "/assets/animals/cassowary/weight.jpg", "Two scales: one shows a cassowary at 59 kilograms (130 pounds), the other shows four 5-year-olds standing together on a scale" %}

A cassowary weighs about 59 kilograms (130 pounds). Picture three or four of your friends standing on one big scale — that's about how much it takes to match one cassowary!

Cassowaries are the rainforest's gardeners. They love fruit that has fallen on the forest floor, and they swallow it whole. Then they wander off, and the seeds they leave behind can grow into brand new trees.

Can you hear that? Cassowaries make a deep, rumbling boom — so low you can almost feel it in your chest. It's one of the lowest sounds any bird makes.

Can you make your lowest, rumbliest sound?

{% figure "/assets/animals/cassowary/baby.jpg", "A cassowary chick with soft striped brown and cream feathers pecking at leaves on the rainforest floor" %}

Baby cassowaries have a surprise, too. They're striped, soft, and fluffy, with no helmet yet — and it's the dad who keeps the eggs warm and looks after the chicks!

Can you stripe-stomp like a cassowary chick?

We always watch this big bird from far away. It likes lots of space, and that's just how a rainforest gardener likes it.
