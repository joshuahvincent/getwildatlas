    African elephants are now treated as two species. The IUCN Red List lists the savanna elephant as Endangered and the forest elephant as Critically Endangered, mostly because of ivory poaching and habitat loss. (The app's older "Vulnerable" label comes from before the 2021 split.) There's a hopeful story: the Sheldrick Wildlife Trust in Kenya has raised more than 320 orphaned elephants and rhinos, caring for each until it is ready to return to the wild.

    Elephants need room to roam. This week, try a family "elephant walk" in your neighborhood, noticing how far you can go and which paths are shared with others. Or learn together about groups that protect elephant habitat. It's a small way to practice making room for wildlife.

    Starters: "If you could talk without words, what would you say?" "What would you share with an elephant friend?"

    If your kid asks *why do people hurt elephants for their tusks?* — this holds up: "Some people want ivory, which is the elephant's tooth, and that's not okay. Lots of people are working hard to protect elephants, and laws and rangers help keep them safe. Elephants need their tusks for digging, eating, and helping family."

African elephants are the biggest animals that walk on land!

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_elephant/cover.jpg", "An African elephant walking across dry golden grassland, kicking up a little dust, with its trunk curled and tusks showing" %}

Can you guess how big? A grown-up elephant stands about 3.3 meters (10 feet 10 inches) tall at the shoulder. You are about 109 centimeters (3 feet 7 inches). So an elephant is about three of you, stacked up!

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_elephant/scale.jpg", "A big African elephant drawn beside a small 5-year-old explorer, with arrows showing the elephant is 3.3 meters tall and 6 meters long" %}

Elephants are heavy, too. A big one can weigh about 5,000 kilograms (11,000 pounds). That's about as much as 271 five-year-olds, all standing on one giant scale together!

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_elephant/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one African elephant weighing about the same as 271 five-year-olds standing together" %}

Can you picture 270 friends on a scale?

Here's a surprise. Elephants can talk in rumbles so low that you can't hear them. Other elephants can feel the rumbles through the ground, with their feet, from far away. Can you stomp softly and listen with your feet?

An elephant's trunk is like a nose and a hand in one. It has tens of thousands of tiny muscle bundles. It can lift a heavy log, and it can pick up a single blade of grass!

Those big ears work like fans. When an elephant flaps them, the air cools it down in the hot sun. Can you flap your "ears" like an elephant?

Elephants love mud baths. The mud helps protect their skin from sunburn and bugs. And those long tusks? They're really giant teeth that never stop growing.

A baby elephant is called a calf. It weighs about as much as five of you, and it can stand up and walk within about half an hour of being born!

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_elephant/baby.jpg", "A baby African elephant standing in golden grass, facing forward, with its big ears out and tall trees behind it" %}

Elephants live in families led by a wise grandma. Friends and family remember each other for a very long time. When they meet, they say hello by wrapping their trunks together.

At mealtime, elephants munch grass, leaves, bark, and fruit for up to 16 hours a day. They drink about a bathtub's worth of water, too. Time for a snack, elephant style!
