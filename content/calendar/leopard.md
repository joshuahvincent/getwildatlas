A leopard's spots are shaped like tiny roses!

{% figure "/assets/animals/leopard/cover.jpg", "A leopard with golden spotted fur walking across a rock in tall dry grass, with hills and trees behind it" %}

Those spots are called rosettes, because they look like little flowers. And every leopard's pattern is different, a bit like a fingerprint. No two leopards look exactly alike.

Leopards are wonderful climbers. A leopard can even carry its dinner up a tree, to keep it safe from other hungry animals. Some leopards are strong enough to haul up an animal heavier than themselves!

Can you tiptoe like a leopard? Leopards walk very, very quietly. They sneak up slowly, so they hardly make a sound.

Leopards can leap more than 6 metres (20 feet) forward. That's longer than a car! They can also jump about 3 metres (10 feet) straight up, which is taller than a door.

{% figure "/assets/animals/leopard/scale.jpg", "A leopard drawn next to a 5-year-old explorer, with the leopard's body about 1.9 metres (6 feet 3 inches) long and 79 centimetres (2 feet 7 inches) tall at the shoulder" %}

On all fours, a leopard stands about 79 centimetres (2 feet 7 inches) tall at the shoulder. That's about up to your chest, if you're five. From nose to tail, it can be about 1.9 metres (6 feet 3 inches) long.

{% figure "/assets/animals/leopard/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one leopard at 60 kilograms (132 pounds) beside four 5-year-olds at 74 kilograms (163 pounds)" %}

A leopard weighs about 60 kilograms (132 pounds). That's about as much as three or four five-year-olds standing together on one big scale.

Can you picture three or four friends on a scale?

Have you heard of a black panther? It's really a leopard with dark fur! Look closely in bright sunlight, and you can still see its spots.

{% figure "/assets/animals/leopard/baby.jpg", "A spotted leopard cub sitting on a rock in warm evening light, looking at the camera" %}

A baby leopard is called a cub. Cubs have thick, fuzzy fur and big, curious eyes. They stay close to their mom while they learn how to climb, hide, and sneak.

Leopards are mostly quiet, but they do make sounds. They make deep growls and a rasping, sawing call that carries across the grass.
