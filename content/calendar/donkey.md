  grownups: |
    The everyday farm donkey isn't rated by the IUCN Red List, the way wild animals are. Its wild ancestor, the African wild ass, is rated Critically Endangered, with only a small number left in the deserts of Eritrea and Ethiopia (likely a few hundred at most), mostly because of hunting and competition for water and grass.

    There's a hopeful story close to home: World Donkey Day began as the idea of one animal scientist, Dr Abdul Raziq Kakar, who noticed how much donkeys help people and how little thanks they get. Today it's marked around the world, and sanctuaries like The Donkey Sanctuary in Ireland care for more than 1,800 donkeys and mules.

    A family hope action: if you visit a farm or sanctuary, spend a minute learning how the donkeys are looked after, like what they eat and where they rest.

    Starters: "What do you think a donkey is saying when it brays?" "How would you say hello to a donkey?"

A donkey's hee-haw can be heard from far, far away!

{% figure "/assets/animals/donkey/cover.jpg", "A gray-brown donkey with long ears trotting across a dusty paddock, kicking up a little dust, in front of a wooden fence", "A donkey on the move" %}

That loud bray is how donkeys talk to each other. And those big ears help them hear a friend calling from very far away.

Donkeys are good at remembering, too. They can remember other donkeys, and places they've been, for many years.

People first tamed donkeys in Africa about five thousand years ago. Since then, donkeys have been helpful friends on farms all around the world.

Donkeys love a good dust bath. They roll on the ground to help keep their skin clean and cool. Can you roll your shoulders like a happy donkey?

{% figure "/assets/animals/donkey/scale.jpg", "A donkey standing next to a smiling girl in pajamas holding a storybook, with arrows showing the donkey is about 1.1 meters tall at the shoulder and 1.6 meters long", "A donkey next to a 5-year-old" %}

How big is a donkey? At the shoulder, it stands about 1.1 meters (3 feet 8 inches) tall. That's just about as tall as a 5-year-old, who is about 1.09 meters (3 feet 7 inches). From nose to tail, a donkey is about 1.6 meters (5 feet 3 inches) long.

{% figure "/assets/animals/donkey/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one donkey weighing 180 kilograms balanced against ten 5-year-olds weighing about the same", "One donkey weighs about as much as 10 five-year-olds" %}

One donkey weighs about 180 kilograms (397 pounds). That's about as much as 10 five-year-olds all standing on one big scale together!

Can you picture 10 friends on one big scale?

{% figure "/assets/animals/donkey/baby.jpg", "A baby donkey foal with big upright ears standing in a green meadow with yellow flowers, with a barn behind it", "A baby donkey, called a foal" %}

A baby donkey is called a foal. Look at those ears! They look huge on such a small face.

Donkeys are careful walkers, with small, tough hooves that are great for rocky paths. They like to look before they step.

Can you tiptoe carefully like a donkey across the room? Now try a big hee-haw!
