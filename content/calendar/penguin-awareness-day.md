  grownups: |
    Emperor penguins are now listed as Endangered (IUCN, April 2026) — a change made because their icy home is warming, and the sea ice is breaking up earlier in spring more often, before chicks have grown the waterproof feathers they need to swim. Satellite counts found the population had dropped by about 10% in just ten years.

    There's a hopeful thread in this story. Scientists at the British Antarctic Survey search satellite pictures for the brown stains left behind by huge groups of penguins — that's how they keep finding new colonies, including four more announced in January 2024, bringing the known total to 66. One famous colony, at Halley Bay, was once feared lost when its sea ice broke apart. It turned out the colony had moved about 30 km along the coast and started again. Scientists can now check on every known emperor penguin colony, every year, from space.

    This week, try being a penguin scientist together: draw a bird's-eye "satellite picture" of a penguin colony — white ice, one brown patch, and lots of tiny dots — then explain to your family how real scientists find penguins this way.

An emperor penguin dad keeps his egg warm on top of his feet for about two months — through the dark Antarctic winter — without eating a single bite.

{% figure "/assets/blog/penguin-awareness-day/cover.jpg", "An emperor penguin swimming underwater beneath the sea ice" %}

Mom lays one egg, then heads out to sea to fish. Dad tucks the egg onto his feet, under a warm flap of belly skin, and keeps it cozy all on his own — through wind, through dark, through some of the coldest cold on Earth. He doesn't eat anything the whole time.

Can you balance a soft toy on your feet without dropping it, like a penguin dad? (Ask a grown-up to help you balance.)

A grown-up emperor penguin stands about as tall as a 5-year-old — a 5-year-old could look one right in the eye!

{% figure "/assets/blog/penguin-awareness-day/scale.jpg", "An emperor penguin standing next to a 5-year-old explorer for scale" %}

It weighs about as much as a 5-year-old and a 2-year-old put together.

{% figure "/assets/blog/penguin-awareness-day/weight.jpg", "A scale comparing an emperor penguin's weight to a 5-year-old and a 2-year-old together" %}

When the chick finally hatches, it's small enough to fit in two cupped hands. If mom isn't back from the sea yet, Dad even makes a special "penguin milk" in his throat for its very first meal.

{% figure "/assets/blog/penguin-awareness-day/baby.jpg", "A fluffy grey emperor penguin chick standing on the ice" %}

Chicks wear fluffy grey feathers, like tiny pajamas, and gather in big groups while both parents go fishing. In a crowd of thousands of penguins, a parent finds its very own chick by listening for one special call.

When it's freezing outside, whole huddles of penguins squeeze together for warmth, and everyone takes a turn in the toasty middle. Can you huddle up close with your family and take turns being in the middle?

Emperor penguins waddle slower than you walk — so sometimes they just flop onto their bellies and slide across the ice instead. Can you slide like a penguin — on a carpet, not on real ice?
