Every single night, an orangutan builds a brand new bed in the treetops!

{% figure "/assets/animals/orangutan/cover.jpg", "An orangutan with long orange-red fur reaching along a branch among green rainforest leaves" %}

Orangutans are great apes with long orange fur. They live high in the rainforest trees of Borneo and Sumatra. They mostly stay up in the trees, though some orangutans in Borneo do walk on the ground.

Each evening, an orangutan bends branches and piles leaves into a cozy nest. It's like making a treehouse bed before bedtime. Then, the next night, it makes another one!

The word "orangutan" comes from the Malay language. It means "person of the forest."

Can you build a cozy nest out of pillows?

{% figure "/assets/animals/orangutan/scale.jpg", "An orangutan standing next to a 5-year-old explorer, with a measurement of 1.4 meters" %}

An orangutan is about 1.4 meters (4 and a half feet) tall when it stands up. That's a little taller than a 5-year-old! But its arms are the real wonder. They can stretch about 2 meters (7 feet) from fingertip to fingertip. That's wider than a grown-up is tall! Those long arms help it swing from tree to tree.

Orangutans are very smart. Some use sticks as tools to get bugs out of tree holes. Some even hold big leaves over their heads like umbrellas when it rains!

Can you hold a pretend umbrella over your head?

{% figure "/assets/animals/orangutan/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one orangutan at 80 kilograms next to a group of five 5-year-olds at 93 kilograms" %}

An orangutan can weigh about 80 kilograms (176 pounds). That's about as much as four or five 5-year-olds standing together on one big scale. It's also the biggest mammal that lives in trees!

Orangutans eat more than 300 different kinds of fruit. Yum!

Can you stretch your arms out as wide as they will go?

{% figure "/assets/animals/orangutan/baby.jpg", "A baby orangutan with big eyes sitting on a mossy branch in the rainforest" %}

A baby orangutan stays close to its mom for up to eight years. That's longer than almost any other animal. She teaches it which fruits to eat and how to climb safely.

Now you know the orangutan's secrets: a new bed every night, super-long arms, and a name that means "person of the forest."
