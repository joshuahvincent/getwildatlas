  whatItCelebrates: |
    Moth Week celebrates moths: the soft, fluttery insects that fly around while most of us are asleep. People all over the world go out to look for them and share what they find.

    Despite its name, National Moth Week is a worldwide event. Anyone can take part, from a backyard to a park.
  whyItMatters: |
    Moths help pollinate flowers at night, and they are food for many other animals. Learning about the moths where you live helps scientists see how nature is doing, and every family sighting can be part of that.
  funFacts:
    - "There are more than 160,000 kinds of moths in the world, many more than butterflies."
    - "Most moths fly at night, and many are drawn to lights."
    - "Many moths drink nectar from flowers, helping to carry pollen as they go."
    - "Some moths are tiny, and some have wings as wide as a dinner plate."
    - "Moths smell with their feathery antennae."
    - "Moths start life as caterpillars, then wrap up and change into adults."
  joinIn: |
    Make a moth-watching night. Hang a white sheet outside, shine a flashlight or porch light on it, and see which moths come to visit. Count them, draw them, or take photos. Families can share sightings for free through citizen-science sites listed on the National Moth Week website. Always ask a grown-up before sharing photos, and stay safe at night.
