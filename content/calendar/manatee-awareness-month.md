  whatItCelebrates: |
    Manatees are big, round, slow-swimming animals that live in warm, shallow water. People sometimes call them sea cows, because they spend their days grazing on underwater plants.

    November is when many Florida manatees begin drifting toward warm springs and cozy warm-water spots, because they don't like the cold. It's a perfect time to learn about them.
  whyItMatters: |
    Manatees move slowly and spend a lot of time near the surface, so they share the water with people in boats. When we learn about them, we can all be better neighbors. The hopeful part: when boaters slow down and give manatees room, manatees can keep right on grazing and snoozing.
  funFacts:
    - "Manatees are plant-eaters. They graze on seagrass and other water plants."
    - "A grown-up manatee can be about as long as a small boat, around 9 or 10 feet (3 meters)."
    - "Manatees are air-breathers. They come up to the surface to take a breath, just like you do."
    - "Their closest living relatives on land include elephants!"
    - "Manatees get chilly easily, so in winter they gather in warm water."
  joinIn: |
    Try some manatee moves at home. Can you float slowly across the room like a sea cow? Can you take a deep breath, then let it out slowly, like a manatee coming up for air?

    If your family ever visits the water in Manatee country, give them space: go slowly, watch from a distance, and never feed or chase them. Wild manatees do best when they're left to graze in peace.
