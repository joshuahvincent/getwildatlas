A great white shark has about 300 teeth — and when one falls out, a new one moves forward to take its place!

{% figure "/assets/animals/great_white_shark/cover.jpg", "A great white shark gliding through blue water, with a grey back, white belly, and a little splash of bubbles behind its tail" %}

Meet the great white shark. It lives in cool oceans all around the world. Its back is grey-blue, so from above it blends in with the dark water. Its belly is white, so from below it blends in with the bright surface. Clever!

Its teeth are shaped like triangles with zigzag edges, a bit like tiny bread knives, all lined up in rows. When a front tooth wears out, the next one steps forward. It never runs out.

This shark also has a super sense of smell. It can notice tiny scents in the water from far away. And it's a fast swimmer — it can zoom in short bursts when it's chasing a snack.

Can you make a zoom sound like a speedy shark? Whoooosh!

Here's a big one. The biggest great whites are about 6 meters (almost 20 feet) long — as long as a minibus. A typical one is a bit smaller, but still much longer than you.

{% figure "/assets/animals/great_white_shark/scale.jpg", "A great white shark drawn next to a 5-year-old diver, with a line showing it is 6 meters (19 feet 9 inches) long" %}

Can you stretch your arms out wide? Now picture more than five of you lying head to toe. That's one very big shark!

It's heavy, too. A big great white can weigh about 1,900 kilograms (4,190 pounds). That's about as much as 103 five-year-olds, all standing on one giant scale together!

{% figure "/assets/animals/great_white_shark/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one great white shark weighing the same as 103 five-year-olds standing on the other side" %}

Baby great whites are called pups. A pup is already about as long as you are when it's born, and it knows how to swim and find food right away. Nobody has to teach it.

{% figure "/assets/animals/great_white_shark/baby.jpg", "A young great white shark pup swimming near the bright surface of the ocean" %}

Great whites can even leap right out of the water! It's called breaching, and it makes a giant splash.

Can you jump like a shark? Whoosh — splash!

Great whites have been swimming in the ocean for a very, very long time, and some live to be 70 years old. They're a big part of keeping the ocean healthy and in balance.
