    African wild dogs are listed as Endangered by the IUCN, with roughly 6,600 left in the wild (only about 1,400 of them adults). They need lots of room, and they're hurt by shrinking habitat, snares, disease, and conflict with people. There's a hopeful story: in Zimbabwe, the group Painted Dog Conservation works with local communities, and the country's wild dog population has grown from about 400 to about 700 dogs since the group began.

African wild dogs vote by sneezing!

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_wild_dog/cover.jpg", "An African wild dog with a spotted brown, black, and white coat running across dry golden grass" %}

When the pack wants to decide whether to go hunting, the dogs sneeze. The more sneezes, the more likely the whole pack heads out. Ah-choo!

Each wild dog has its own splashy coat of brown, black, white, and yellow patches. No two coats match, so they're a bit like fingerprints. Some people call them painted dogs.

These dogs are also fast. They can run about 60 kilometres per hour (37 miles per hour). That's faster than a car in a neighborhood! And they work as a team, taking turns chasing, so they catch their food most of the time they try.

Can you tap your four fingers on your knee? Wild dogs have only four toes on each foot, not five like most dogs.

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_wild_dog/scale.jpg", "An African wild dog standing next to a 5-year-old child, with the dog about 70 centimetres tall at the shoulder" %}

A wild dog stands about 70 centimetres (2 feet 4 inches) tall at the shoulder. That's about as high as a 5-year-old's tummy! It's about 1.3 metres (4 feet 4 inches) long from nose to tail.

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_wild_dog/weight.jpg", "A scale showing an African wild dog at 25 kilograms next to a 5-year-old child at about 19 kilograms" %}

A wild dog weighs about 25 kilograms (55 pounds). That's a little more than a 5-year-old.

Can you make a squeaky, twittery greeting? When wild dogs say hello, they chirp and squeak almost like birds.

Wild dog families are called packs, usually with six to twenty dogs, and sometimes more. Everyone helps out. The pack shares food with the puppies, and with dogs who are older or not feeling well.

{% figure "/assets/animals/african_wild_dog/baby.jpg", "A wild dog puppy sitting in dry grass with big round ears" %}

Wild dog puppies are born in a den underground, snug and safe. They grow up with a whole pack of helpers. And those big, round ears? They help the dogs hear far away, and they help them stay cool in the hot sun.
