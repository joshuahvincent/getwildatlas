    The gorilla is listed as Critically Endangered, mostly from habitat loss, hunting, and disease. There's a hopeful story: mountain gorillas, one kind of gorilla, fell to about 242 in 1981. Park rangers, researchers, and local communities have protected them ever since, and their numbers have now passed 1,000. The IUCN has moved them from Critically Endangered to Endangered. The Dian Fossey Gorilla Fund calls them the only wild ape population known to be growing.

Gorillas build a brand new bed every single night!

{% figure "/assets/animals/gorilla/cover.jpg", "A big dark gorilla walking on its knuckles through a green forest with ferns and misty mountains behind it" %}

A gorilla bends branches and leaves into a cozy nest. Sometimes it's on the ground. Sometimes it's up in a tree. In the morning, the nest is left behind, and tonight the gorilla builds another one.

Gorillas are the biggest apes in the whole world. They live in the forests of central Africa.

Gorillas are gentle giants. They mostly eat plants — leaves, stems, bamboo, and fruit. And here's a happy surprise: when gorillas are enjoying a good meal, they hum and sing!

Can you hum a happy, munching song, just like a gorilla?

{% figure "/assets/animals/gorilla/scale.jpg", "A gorilla about 1.7 meters tall standing on all fours next to a 5-year-old explorer, with a measuring arrow between them" %}

A big gorilla standing up is about 1.7 meters (5 feet 7 inches) tall. That's about 60 centimeters (2 feet) taller than a 5-year-old, and about as tall as many grown-ups.

A big gorilla can weigh around 180 kilograms (397 pounds). That's about as much as ten 5-year-olds, all standing on one big scale together!

{% figure "/assets/animals/gorilla/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one gorilla weighing 180 kilograms balanced against ten 5-year-olds weighing 185 kilograms" %}

Can you count to ten friends on that scale?

A gorilla family is called a troop. It's usually led by one big male called a silverback. When a boy gorilla grows up, silver hair grows on his back — that's how he gets his name. The silverback looks after everyone in his family.

Sometimes a gorilla pats its chest with a *pa-pa-pa* sound. It's one way a gorilla says, "I'm here!"

Can you pat your chest gently, like a gorilla? Not too hard!

Baby gorillas stay very close to their moms. A baby rides on its mom's back until it's about three years old.

{% figure "/assets/animals/gorilla/baby.jpg", "A baby gorilla sitting on a mossy log with its arms folded, with soft golden forest light behind it" %}

Gorillas talk with soft grunts. They hoot when they're happy. And if they need to say "please stay back," they can roar — but mostly, they're calm and peaceful.

Tonight, when you snuggle into bed, think of a gorilla building its leafy nest. Sweet dreams!
