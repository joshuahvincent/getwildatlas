Capybaras are the biggest rodents in the whole world — and the friendliest!

{% figure "/assets/animals/capybara/cover.jpg", "A brown capybara standing on a muddy, root-covered bank beside calm water, with green plants behind it" %}

A capybara is a rodent, like a mouse or a guinea pig, just much, much bigger. It lives near rivers, lakes, and wetlands in South America. It's so calm and gentle that birds, and even monkeys, sit on its back like a comfy chair!

Can you sit as still and calm as a capybara?

{% figure "/assets/animals/capybara/scale.jpg", "An illustrated capybara standing beside the Wild Atlas explorer kid, with arrows showing the capybara is about 1.3 meters long and about 60 centimeters tall" %}

A capybara is about 1.3 meters (4 feet 4 inches) long. That's longer than a 5-year-old is tall! At the shoulder, it stands about 60 centimeters (2 feet) high, which reaches about up to your belly.

{% figure "/assets/animals/capybara/weight.jpg", "Two scales side by side: one capybara at 60 kilograms (132 pounds) and four illustrated 5-year-olds at 74 kilograms (163 pounds)" %}

A grown-up capybara weighs about 60 kilograms (132 pounds). That's about as much as three or four 5-year-olds, all standing on one big scale!

It's related to guinea pigs, but it's about 50 times bigger.

Capybaras love the water. Their feet are a little bit webbed, and their eyes, ears, and nose sit high on top of their head. That means a capybara can swim, or float with just its nose sticking out, and still see and hear everything. It can even hold its breath for up to five minutes.

Can you hold your breath and count slowly? Try it for just a few seconds. Don't go for five minutes!

{% figure "/assets/animals/capybara/baby.jpg", "A young capybara standing on a mossy log among green water plants, with a river behind it" %}

A baby capybara can run and swim just hours after it's born. Capybaras live together in a herd, usually 10 to 20 of them, and they chat in squeaks, barks, purrs, and whistles.

A capybara's front teeth never stop growing. So it munches grass all day, and that keeps its teeth just the right size. It also takes mud baths to cool off on hot days.

Can you squeak and whistle like a capybara?
