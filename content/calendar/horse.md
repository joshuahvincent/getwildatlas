Horses can sleep standing up!

{% figure "/assets/animals/horse/cover.jpg", "A chestnut horse with a flowing mane trotting across a grassy field in front of a barn", "A horse on the move" %}

A horse can lock its knees so it doesn't tip over. That's how it takes a nap on its feet. For a deep sleep, though, it still lies down, just like you.

Horses have been our partners for thousands of years. People first tamed them on the grasslands of Eurasia more than five thousand years ago.

Horses also talk without words. They use their ears, eyes, and nostrils to show how they feel. A horse might neigh to call a friend, or snort when it's excited.

Can you make a gentle neigh like a horse? Try one!

{% figure "/assets/animals/horse/scale.jpg", "A brown horse standing next to a small boy in pajamas, with arrows showing the horse is about 1.6 meters tall at the shoulder and 2.4 meters long", "A horse next to a 5-year-old" %}

A horse is big! At the shoulder, it stands about 1.6 meters (5 feet 2 inches) tall. That's a lot taller than a 5-year-old, who is about 1.1 meters (3 feet 7 inches).

{% figure "/assets/animals/horse/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one horse weighing 500 kilograms balanced against 28 five-year-olds weighing about the same", "One horse weighs about as much as 28 five-year-olds" %}

One horse weighs about 500 kilograms (1,100 pounds). That's about as much as 28 five-year-olds all standing on one big scale together!

Can you picture 28 friends on one big scale?

Here's a surprise. A horse's hoof is made of the same stuff as your fingernails! It's like a very big, very tough toenail.

{% figure "/assets/animals/horse/baby.jpg", "A young foal with a white stripe on its face standing on a grassy field dotted with dandelions in front of a barn", "A foal, a baby horse" %}

A baby horse is called a foal. A foal can stand up and walk within a couple of hours of being born. That's very fast! It needs its long legs ready, because horses are always on the move.

Horses also have big, round eyes, among the biggest of any land animal. And they have good memories. A horse can remember people it hasn't seen in years.

Can you remember the name of someone you haven't seen in a long time?

Wild horse cousins live in groups called herds, and the herd members take turns keeping watch while the others rest. Isn't that a good way to look after each other?
