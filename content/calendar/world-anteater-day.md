  whatItCelebrates: |
    Anteaters live in Central and South America. They use their long, bendy snouts to sniff out ants and termites, then slurp them up with a very long, sticky tongue.

    This day is for learning about all the anteater kinds, from the big giant anteater to the small ones that climb trees.
  whyItMatters: |
    Anteaters help keep ant and termite numbers in balance, and they're a special part of the grasslands and forests where they live. Scientists who study them are working to learn more and to look after their homes. The more kids know about anteaters, the more friends they have.
  funFacts:
    - "Anteaters have no teeth. They squish their food with their tongue and tummy."
    - "A giant anteater's tongue can be about 60 centimeters (2 feet) long."
    - "They flick their tongue in and out very, very fast, around 150 times a minute."
    - "A giant anteater's bushy tail is like a big blanket that it can curl over itself to sleep."
    - "Baby anteaters hitch a ride, clinging to their mom's back."
