    Axolotls are listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. In the wild they live only in a few waterways near Mexico City, where polluted water, shrinking habitat, and invasive fish have made life hard. Many more axolotls live in labs and homes than in the wild — which is part of why wild ones need protecting.

    There's a hopeful story: researchers released 18 radio-tagged, captive-bred axolotls into restored and artificial wetlands in the Xochimilco area, then tracked them to learn how they get along in the wild. The study is helping conservationists work out how to bring axolotls back to cleaner, restored waterways.

    A family hope action: axolotls need clean water. This week, try picking up litter near a pond or stream, and pour only water (not paint or cleaning liquids) down outdoor drains. It's the same idea as Xochimilco's restoration: healthy water means healthy animals.

Meet the axolotl, the smiling water creature with a frilly pink crown!

{% figure "/assets/animals/axolotl/cover.jpg", "A pale pink axolotl with feathery gills floating just above a sandy bottom, with green water plants behind it" %}

That frilly crown is made of gills. They're how an axolotl breathes underwater. Its mouth curves up at the corners, so it always looks like it's smiling at you.

Here's the biggest surprise. If an axolotl loses a leg or a tail, it can grow a new one! Scientists study axolotls to learn how living things heal and regrow.

An axolotl is a kind of salamander. Most salamanders grow up and crawl onto land. Not the axolotl. It keeps its baby shape and stays in the water its whole life.

Can you wiggle like an axolotl? Make your fingers feathery and sway them around your head, like gills!

An axolotl is about 25 centimeters (10 inches) long. That's about as long as your forearm, from your elbow to your fingertips.

{% figure "/assets/animals/axolotl/scale.jpg", "A tiny axolotl drawn next to a child explorer, with a zoomed-in picture of the axolotl and a label saying it is about 25 centimeters (10 inches) long" %}

It's very light, too. An axolotl weighs about 100 grams (about 3.5 ounces), a bit like a small apple. A 5-year-old weighs about as much as 185 axolotls!

{% figure "/assets/animals/axolotl/weight.jpg", "A scale with 185 tiny axolotls on one side and a 5-year-old child on the other, both weighing the same" %}

Can you picture 185 axolotls on one big scale?

Axolotls start out very small. Mom lays eggs and sticks them to water plants. When the tiny babies hatch, they are only about as long as your fingernail is wide.

{% figure "/assets/animals/axolotl/baby.jpg", "A dark, speckled axolotl with red gills resting on a mossy log in green water", "Axolotls in the wild are often dark and speckled, which helps them blend in." %}

An axolotl doesn't chew its dinner. It opens its mouth and gulps in worms and bugs in one slurp. It finds food by sniffing and feeling tiny wiggles in the water.

Can you make a slurpy gulp sound?

In the wild, axolotls live only near Mexico City, in Mexico. Their name comes from an old Mexican story about a water spirit. Every year on February 1, people celebrate Axolotl Day and help look after their watery home.
