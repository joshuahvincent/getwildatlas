Dolphins sleep with half their brain awake!

{% figure "/assets/animals/dolphin/cover.jpg", "A gray dolphin leaping out of the blue ocean with water splashing off its tail" %}

A dolphin is not a fish. It's a mammal, like you, so it has to swim up to the top to breathe air. If it fell fast asleep, it might forget to breathe!

So a dolphin switches off just half its brain at a time. The other half stays awake to keep breathing and stay safe. Sleepy dolphins even rest with one eye open.

Dolphins also have names — whistle names! Each dolphin has its own special whistle that works like a name, and friends can call to each other with it.

They click, too. Dolphins make clicking sounds that bounce off things and come back, so they can find fish even in dark water. It's like seeing with sound.

Can you make a dolphin click with your tongue? Can you whistle your own whistle name?

{% figure "/assets/animals/dolphin/scale.jpg", "A dolphin about 2.5 meters long next to a 5-year-old in a snorkel mask, showing the dolphin is much longer" %}

A bottlenose dolphin is about 2.5 meters (8 feet 3 inches) long. That's about twice as long as a 5-year-old is tall! Dolphins can swim up to about 30 kilometers (19 miles) per hour, and they often leap out of the water when they swim fast. Scientists think that might help them save energy.

{% figure "/assets/animals/dolphin/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one dolphin weighing 200 kilograms balanced against 11 five-year-olds" %}

A dolphin weighs about 200 kilograms (441 pounds). That's as much as 11 five-year-olds, all standing on one big scale together!

Can you picture 11 friends on a scale?

{% figure "/assets/animals/dolphin/baby.jpg", "A young dolphin gliding underwater over a sandy sea floor in clear turquoise water" %}

Baby dolphins are called calves. A calf stays close to its mom for several years, learning how to find food and how to be a dolphin.

Dolphins live in groups called pods, and they look out for each other. They've even been seen helping a friend who's hurt by gently pushing it up to the surface to breathe.

Dolphins can also recognize themselves in a mirror. That's something only a few animals can do!
