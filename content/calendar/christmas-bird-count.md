  whatItCelebrates: |
    The Christmas Bird Count is a big citizen-science project, which means ordinary people help scientists by noticing and counting what they see. Volunteers count the birds in a set area on one day.

    Over more than a century, all those counts have grown into one of the longest-running bird surveys anywhere.
  whyItMatters: |
    When scientists can compare bird numbers year after year, they learn which birds are doing well and which might need a helping hand. Every kid who counts a chickadee at the window can be part of that big picture.
  funFacts:
    - "The count has been going since Christmas Day 1900, more than 120 years."
    - "Counters can be new bird watchers or experts, and kids can come along with a grown-up."
    - "Many birds stay put through winter, so you may spot cardinals, jays, and robins in the cold."
    - "A bird's colors and songs can help you tell who's who."
    - "You don't need fancy gear. Your eyes, ears, and a pencil are enough."
  joinIn: |
    Anyone can join. Visit Audubon's page below to find a count near you, and a local organiser can tell you how to take part, often from a park or even your own yard. Counts run for a few weeks around the holidays, and each one has a set day.

    No count nearby? Try your own five-minute bird watch: sit by a window, tally the birds you see on paper, and see how your list changes next week.
