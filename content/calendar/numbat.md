  grownups: |
    In July 2026 the IUCN Red List moved the numbat from Endangered to Near Threatened. That's a real comeback story: in the late 1970s only about 300 were thought to remain, and today there are an estimated 2,000 to 3,000 adults. The credit goes to fenced safe areas, work to keep feral cats and foxes away, habitat protection, and a Perth Zoo program that has released more than 300 zoo-born numbats into the wild. It isn't a finished story, though — numbats still live in only about 1% of their old range and are still listed as endangered under Australian law, so the work continues.

A numbat has a tongue about as long as your finger — and it uses it to slurp up termites!

{% figure "/assets/animals/numbat/cover.jpg", "A striped numbat with a bushy tail trotting across a dry, leafy floor in a sunny eucalyptus woodland" %}

A numbat is a small striped animal from Western Australia. Bold white stripes run across its reddish-brown back, and its tail is big and bushy.

Here's a surprise: it hunts in the daytime. Most of the small marsupials around it are fast asleep, but the numbat is out in the sunshine, sniffing along fallen logs.

What does it hunt? Termites. A numbat can eat up to 20,000 termites in one day! Its sticky tongue is about 10 centimeters (4 inches) long — nearly half as long as its whole body — so it can reach right into tiny tunnels.

Can you stick out your tongue as far as you can? Now imagine it that long and sticky!

{% figure "/assets/animals/numbat/scale.jpg", "A tiny numbat, 22 centimeters (9 inches) long, standing on the ground next to a 5-year-old child in an explorer outfit who is much taller" %}

A numbat is small. Its body is only about 22 centimeters (9 inches) long, plus its bushy tail. Next to a 5-year-old, who is about 109 centimeters (3 feet 7 inches) tall, a numbat looks very tiny!

It's light, too. A numbat weighs about half a kilogram (1 pound). A 5-year-old weighs about 18.5 kilograms (41 pounds) — as much as 37 numbats all together!

{% figure "/assets/animals/numbat/weight.jpg", "A scale holding 37 numbats balanced against a scale holding one 5-year-old child, both reading 19 kilograms (41 pounds)" %}

Can you count how many numbats fit on one scale?

At night, a numbat tucks itself into a hollow log, and it may plug the doorway with its furry bottom. Snug!

Numbat mothers don't have a pouch. Their babies cling to her furry tummy while they grow.

{% figure "/assets/animals/numbat/baby.jpg", "A numbat with a striped back and a ringed tail walking along a fallen log beside a small reddish termite mound in a sunny woodland" %}

Numbats are quiet animals. If something startles one, it might make a soft clicking sound: tut-tut-tut.

Can you tut-tut like a numbat? Can you tiptoe along like one, with a stripey back and a bushy tail?

There aren't many numbats in the world, but lots of people are working to look after them. And every first Saturday of November, they celebrate on World Numbat Day.
