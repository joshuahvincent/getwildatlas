    Harp seals are listed as **Near Threatened** by the IUCN (2025), up from Least Concern, mainly because the sea ice they need for giving birth, nursing, and resting is melting as the Arctic warms about four times faster than the rest of the world. There are still millions of harp seals — one of the most numerous seals on Earth — and there's a hopeful precedent: by the late 1960s, heavy hunting had cut the Northwest Atlantic herd to about 2 million, but in 1987 Canada banned the commercial hunting of whitecoat pups, and the herd grew back into the millions. Hunting of older seals continues legally today, so the story isn't finished, but it shows that when people change the rules, seal numbers can bounce back. And in March 2026, a young harp seal that washed up sick on a Massachusetts beach was nursed back to health at Mystic Aquarium and released to the ocean just two days before this year's International Day of the Seal.

A baby harp seal is born on floating ice in a fluffy white coat. In less than two weeks, drinking its mom's super-rich milk, it grows to three times its birth weight!

{% figure "/assets/blog/international-seal-day-2027/cover.jpg", "A harp seal swimming through clear Arctic water" %}

A brand-new harp seal pup is already about as heavy as a big bag of dog food, and almost as long as you are tall. Harp seal milk is super creamy — much, much richer than the milk in your fridge — and the pup gains weight fast. By the time it's ready to be on its own, it weighs as much as two five-year-olds.

Can you guess how a grown-up harp seal got its name? Look closely at its back — there's a dark, curvy mark shaped just like a harp!

{% figure "/assets/blog/international-seal-day-2027/scale.jpg", "A grown-up harp seal lying beside a 5-year-old for scale" %}

A grown-up harp seal is about as long as one and a half kids lying head to toe in a row, and it weighs about as much as seven five-year-olds put together.

{% figure "/assets/blog/international-seal-day-2027/weight.jpg", "A scale showing a harp seal's weight compared to seven five-year-olds" %}

Can you picture seven friends on one big scale?

Harp seals can hold their breath for about as long as a whole cartoon episode, and dive about 400 meters (1,300 feet) down — deeper than most skyscrapers are tall. Underwater, they're chatty too — they warble, trill, and chirp, and pups call out to their moms.

{% figure "/assets/blog/international-seal-day-2027/baby.jpg", "A fluffy white harp seal pup lying on snowy sea ice" %}

Baby harp seals wait on the ice in their fluffy white coat until they're big and strong enough to swim. After a few weeks, the fluff falls away, and a smooth grey coat is underneath.

Can you curl up small and fluffy like a baby seal on the ice? Can you wiggle across the floor like a seal, without using your hands?

Harp seals live in the icy North Atlantic and Arctic Ocean, near Canada, Greenland, Norway, and Russia — and every March, more fluffy white pups are born on the sea ice. If you ever spot a seal pup alone on a beach, the kind thing to do is watch quietly from far away and tell a grown-up.
