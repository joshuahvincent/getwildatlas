  whatItCelebrates: |
    The Big Garden Birdwatch is a citizen-science project, which means ordinary people help scientists by noticing things. For one hour, you watch the birds in your garden, yard, or a nearby park and count the ones you see.

    It started as a way to get children excited about birds, and it has grown into one of the biggest bird counts in the world.
  whyItMatters: |
    When lots of people count birds in the same way every year, scientists can see which birds are visiting more often and which are visiting less. That helps everyone find the best ways to give birds a hand, and your hour of watching is a real part of it.
  funFacts:
    - "The Big Garden Birdwatch has been going since 1979."
    - "You only need one hour. Pick a cosy spot and watch quietly."
    - "You count the most birds of each kind that you see at the same moment, so you don't count the same bird twice."
    - "A bird feeder, some water, or a bit of shrub can bring more visitors."
    - "Birds are often easiest to spot early in the morning."
  joinIn: |
    The Birdwatch is free. Visit the RSPB page below for the weekend dates and a guide to common garden birds, then watch for one hour from a window, your yard, or a park. Write down the birds you see, and ask a grown-up to help you share your count with the RSPB if you'd like to.

    Can't join the weekend? Try your own five-minute watch and tally what visits. Do it again next week and compare.
