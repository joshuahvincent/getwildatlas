    Most of the world's albatross species are listed as threatened by the IUCN, and the wandering albatross, with the longest wings of all, is Vulnerable. The biggest dangers are fishing hooks and nets at sea, plastic, and rats or mice that eat eggs and chicks on nesting islands. There's a hopeful story: South Georgia, a remote island where albatrosses nest, was declared free of rats in 2018 after a huge, years-long effort, and surveys have since found more nesting seabirds and songbirds there.

An albatross can glide over the ocean for hours without flapping its wings once!

{% figure "/assets/animals/albatross/cover.jpg", "An albatross with long dark wings gliding low over deep blue ocean waves, with rocky islands far behind it" %}

How? It locks its wings wide open and rides the wind, like a kite with feathers. It might fly more than 1,000 kilometers (about 620 miles) in a single day.

Its wings are the longest of any bird. The biggest albatross can stretch them about 3.4 meters (11 feet) wide. That's about three 5-year-olds, lying head to toe!

{% figure "/assets/animals/albatross/scale.jpg", "An albatross with its wings spread wide beside a small explorer girl, with a measuring line showing the wingspan is 3.4 meters (11 feet 2 inches)" %}

Can you stretch your arms out like wings? Now imagine them three times longer.

Here's a surprise. Albatrosses have a great sense of smell, and they can sniff out dinner from far away. They also drink salty seawater, then sneeze the extra salt out through tubes on their beaks.

An albatross weighs about 8 kilograms (18 pounds), about as much as a small watermelon. A 5-year-old weighs about as much as two or three albatrosses.

{% figure "/assets/animals/albatross/weight.jpg", "Three albatrosses standing on one scale beside a 5-year-old explorer on another, comparing their weights" %}

A young albatross may spend years at sea without touching land. Albatrosses come ashore mostly to nest. Many pairs stay together for life, and each year they meet again and dance, spreading their wings and clacking their beaks.

Can you clack your teeth like a beak, and bob your head hello?

An albatross chick is a fluffy white ball. It stays in its nest for almost a whole year while its parents bring food from far across the sea. Then one day, it spreads its wings, and off it glides.

{% figure "/assets/animals/albatross/baby.jpg", "A fluffy white albatross chick sitting in its grassy nest on a clifftop above the ocean" %}

Some albatrosses live to be 70 years old. Can you imagine flying for that long?
