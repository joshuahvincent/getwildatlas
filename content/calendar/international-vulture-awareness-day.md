  whatItCelebrates: |
    Vultures are big birds that glide for hours on warm rising air, hardly flapping at all. Today is a day to notice them and learn what they do.

    The white-backed vulture lives on the grasslands of Africa. It looks for food from high in the sky, then shares the meal with its flock.
  whyItMatters: |
    Vultures are nature's clean-up crew. By eating animals that have already died, they help keep the land clean. Some kinds of vultures are having a hard time, and people around the world are working to help them. Every person who learns about vultures is part of that good news.
  funFacts:
    - "Vultures can glide high in the sky for a long time by riding warm air that rises from the ground."
    - "They have excellent eyesight, so they can spot food from far away."
    - "A group of vultures circling in the sky is sometimes called a kettle."
    - "Vultures have very strong stomach acid, which helps them eat food that other animals could not."
    - "Many vultures have bare heads, which helps them stay clean after a meal."
    - "White-backed vultures often nest in tall trees on African grasslands."
  joinIn: |
    Look up! Next time you're outside, watch for big birds gliding in the sky. Draw a vulture with a very wide wingspan, or pretend to be one by stretching your arms and gliding slowly around the room. You can also look up your local wildlife group to see how people near you help birds.
