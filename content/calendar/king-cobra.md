The king cobra is the longest venomous snake in the world!

{% figure "/assets/animals/king_cobra/cover.jpg", "A king cobra with its head raised, coiled on a mossy branch beside a forest stream" %}

Just how long? A big king cobra can stretch about 4.5 meters (14 feet 10 inches). That's about as long as a small car — or four 5-year-olds lying head to toe!

{% figure "/assets/animals/king_cobra/scale.jpg", "A king cobra stretched out next to a 5-year-old explorer; the snake is many times longer than the child is tall, with a label reading 4.5 m (14 ft 10 in)" %}

Can you stretch your arms out wide and picture it? Now picture even more!

Here's a name surprise. The king cobra's scientific name means "snake-eater," because it dines mostly on other snakes. That's how it got the "king" in its name!

When a king cobra feels worried, it lifts the front of its body way up and spreads out a wide hood. It can raise its head so high that it could look a grown-up right in the eye. Then it makes a low, rumbly hiss that sounds a little like a growl.

Can you make a low, growly hiss? Try it nice and slow: *hhhhhhhhh*.

Even with that long body, a king cobra isn't very heavy. One weighs about 9 kilograms (20 pounds) — around half as much as a 5-year-old.

{% figure "/assets/animals/king_cobra/weight.jpg", "A scale showing three king cobras weighing 27 kilograms together, next to one 5-year-old weighing 19 kilograms" %}

Here's the best part. Most snakes don't build anything — but the king cobra is one of the only snakes that makes a nest! The mother piles up leaves into a cozy mound and lays her eggs inside. Then she stays close by and guards them for about two to three months.

{% figure "/assets/animals/king_cobra/baby.jpg", "A baby king cobra with bright yellow bands curled on the leafy forest floor" %}

When the babies hatch, they're already little snake-eaters in the making, with bold yellow stripes across their dark bodies, curled up on the leafy forest floor.

King cobras live in the forests and swamps of Asia. They're great swimmers, and they can even climb trees. And like all wild animals, they like their space — so we say hello with our eyes and our imaginations, from far away.
