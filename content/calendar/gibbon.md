Gibbons sing songs that echo across the whole rainforest!

{% figure "/assets/animals/gibbon/cover.jpg", "A pale gibbon hanging by one long arm from a branch in a green rainforest" %}

A gibbon is a small ape that lives high up in the treetops of Southeast Asia. Mom and dad gibbon sing together in loud, whooping duets. The songs get higher and higher, and you can hear them far across the forest. The songs also tell other gibbon families, "This part of the forest is ours!"

Gibbons are apes, not monkeys. That means they have no tail at all.

Here's another surprise: gibbons have very long arms. They swing hand over hand from branch to branch, which is called brachiating. It looks almost like flying! Their bendy wrists let their hands swivel, so they don't have to slow down.

Can you swing your arms like a gibbon? Try it while you stay safely on the ground!

{% figure "/assets/animals/gibbon/scale.jpg", "A gibbon standing next to a 5-year-old explorer for scale; the gibbon is about up to the child's tummy" %}

Standing up, a gibbon is about 75 centimeters (2 feet 6 inches) tall. That's shorter than a 5-year-old, who is about 109 centimeters (3 feet 7 inches). A gibbon is light, too. It weighs only about 6 kilograms (13 pounds) — less than a big house cat. It takes about four gibbons to weigh a bit more than one 5-year-old!

{% figure "/assets/animals/gibbon/weight.jpg", "A scale showing four gibbons weighing 24 kilograms, next to one 5-year-old weighing 19 kilograms" %}

Being light helps gibbons swing without breaking the branches. And gibbons can leap across very wide gaps between trees. They almost never come down to the ground — the treetops are their whole world.

Can you imagine a world where your floor is a branch?

Baby gibbons hold tight to their mom's tummy while she swings through the trees.

{% figure "/assets/animals/gibbon/baby.jpg", "A young gibbon sitting on a mossy branch holding a green piece of fruit" %}

Now it's your turn. Can you whoop like a gibbon? Start low, then go higher and higher. Happy International Gibbon Day!
