  whatItCelebrates: |
    Turkey vultures are big dark birds with bare red heads. In mid-March they come back to Hinckley, Ohio, after a winter away, and the town cheers them home. They glide on the wind for hours with hardly a flap of their wings.

    Turkey vultures are nature's clean-up crew. They find food that has already died and tidy it away, which keeps the countryside clean.
  whyItMatters: |
    Turkey vultures are helpful neighbours. By tidying up, they help keep the land clean and healthy. They remind us that every animal has a job, even the ones we don't always think to cheer for.
  funFacts:
    - "Turkey vultures hold their wings in a shallow V and wobble a little as they glide."
    - "Their wings stretch about 1.7 metres (5½ feet) from tip to tip, which is why they can soar so easily."
    - "Turkey vultures have a very good sense of smell, which is rare for birds."
    - "Their heads have no feathers, which helps keep them clean."
    - "They don't build nests. They lay eggs on the ground in a quiet cave, hollow log, or rocky ledge."
