A pangolin is covered in scales from head to tail, and they're made of the same stuff as your fingernails!

{% figure "/assets/blog/world-pangolin-day-2027/cover.jpg", "A pangolin walking across dry savanna grass at golden hour" %}

Every scale, stacked over the next like a pinecone, is made of keratin — the same material as your fingernails and hair. The pangolin is the only mammal in the world covered all over in scales.

This one is a Chinese pangolin, one of eight kinds of pangolin. It lives in forests across Asia, from Nepal to China and Taiwan, tiptoeing out at night to sniff out ants and termites.

Here's a strange thing: a pangolin has no teeth at all. It scoops up ants with a long, sticky tongue — so long it's tucked away all the way down inside its chest! Then it swallows a few tiny stones, which help mash up its dinner in its tummy.

{% figure "/assets/blog/world-pangolin-day-2027/scale.jpg", "A curled-up pangolin beside the Wild Atlas child explorer for scale" %}

A grown-up Chinese pangolin's body is a bit less than half as tall as a 5-year-old — about 45 centimeters (1 foot 6 inches) — plus a long tail behind it.

{% figure "/assets/blog/world-pangolin-day-2027/weight.jpg", "A scale with four pangolins compared to a scale with one 5-year-old child" %}

Can you picture four pangolins on one big scale? Together, they'd weigh about 24 kilograms (53 pounds) — a little more than one 5-year-old, at about 19 kilograms (41 pounds).

When a pangolin feels scared, it doesn't run. It curls into a tight, tight ball, so only its tough scales show. Almost nothing can bite through a ball like that.

{% figure "/assets/blog/world-pangolin-day-2027/baby.jpg", "A young pangolin gripping a mossy log in the forest" %}

A mother pangolin usually has just one baby. Its scales start out soft but harden within days, so until then, the baby rides safely on its mom's tail.

Can you curl into the smallest, tightest ball you can, just like a pangolin? Can you tiptoe around the room like a shy, nighttime pangolin, sniffing for ants?
