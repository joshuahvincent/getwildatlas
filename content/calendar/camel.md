  grownups: |
    Camels come in two main kinds: the one-humped dromedary and the two-humped Bactrian. Almost all of them live with people, as working and farm animals, so the IUCN Red List doesn't give them a conservation status in the usual way. Their wild cousin, the wild Bactrian camel, is a separate species that lives only in remote parts of China and Mongolia. It is listed as Endangered, with around a thousand adults left. That's an improvement: in October 2025 the IUCN moved it down from Critically Endangered, partly because of new information and partly because protected areas such as Great Gobi National Park in Mongolia are helping. It still needs a lot of care, since water, mining, and mixing with domestic camels remain worries.

    A small family hope action: camels survive on very little water, and we can be careful with water too. This week, try turning off the tap while brushing teeth, or collecting a little cool bathwater or rinse water to give a plant a drink. Talk about how desert animals make a little go a long way.

A camel's hump is not full of water. It's full of fat — like a pantry that you wear on your back!

{% figure "/assets/animals/camel/cover.jpg", "A one-humped camel striding across sandy desert under a blue sky" %}

When a camel can't find food, its body turns that fat into energy. That's one reason a camel can go more than a week without a drink. And when it does find water, it can slurp up a huge amount in just a few minutes.

Camels have other desert super-powers too. They can close their nostrils tight to keep out blowing sand. Thick eyelashes sweep sand away from their eyes. And their wide, flat feet spread out like snowshoes, so they don't sink into the soft dunes.

Camels come in two kinds. The dromedary has one hump. The Bactrian has two!

How big is a camel? Very big. A camel stands about 1.9 meters (6 feet 3 inches) tall at the hump. That's much taller than you, and a little taller than most grown-ups. From nose to tail it's about 3 meters (almost 10 feet) long.

{% figure "/assets/animals/camel/scale.jpg", "A two-humped camel standing beside a small child for scale, with arrows showing its height and length" %}

Can you stand on tiptoe and stretch your arm up high? Is a camel still taller?

A camel weighs about as much as 33 five-year-olds, all standing on one big scale together. That's about 600 kilograms (1,323 pounds).

{% figure "/assets/animals/camel/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one camel weighing about the same as 33 five-year-olds standing together" %}

Here's a surprise. Baby camels are born without humps! A baby camel, called a calf, has soft, fluffy fur and long wobbly legs. The humps grow later, as the calf gets bigger.

{% figure "/assets/animals/camel/baby.jpg", "A young camel calf with long thin legs standing on a sand dune" %}

People and camels have been friends for thousands of years. Camels have carried families, food, and tents across hot, dry lands.

Can you walk across the room with slow, swaying camel steps? Can you squeeze your nose shut like a camel's nostrils when the sand blows? What sound do you think a camel makes? It's a low grunt, and sometimes a big bellow. Try it!
