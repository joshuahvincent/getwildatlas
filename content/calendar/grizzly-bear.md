A grizzly bear can sniff out a snack from very, very far away!

{% figure "/assets/animals/grizzly_bear/cover.jpg", "A big brown grizzly bear walking through a forest with rocks and pine trees behind it" %}

A grizzly's nose is much stronger than yours. It can smell berries, fish, and tasty roots from a long, long way off. Sniff, sniff, sniff!

See the big hump on its back? That's pure muscle. It helps the grizzly dig for roots and climb up hills.

Grizzlies are fast, too. A grizzly can run as fast as a horse, up to 55 kilometers per hour (about 34 miles per hour). Can you run like a bear? Stomp, stomp, stomp!

{% figure "/assets/animals/grizzly_bear/scale.jpg", "A grizzly bear standing beside a 5-year-old explorer. The bear's shoulders reach about as high as the child's head, and the labels show the bear is 2.1 meters long and about 1 meter tall at the shoulder" %}

On all fours, a grizzly's shoulders come up to about as high as you are: around 1 meter (3 feet 4 inches). When it stands up on its back legs, it's nearly twice as tall as you!

A big grizzly weighs about 270 kilograms (595 pounds). That's about as much as 15 five-year-olds all standing on one big scale together!

{% figure "/assets/animals/grizzly_bear/weight.jpg", "A scale showing one grizzly bear weighing 270 kilograms balanced against a scale holding fifteen 5-year-olds" %}

Can you picture 15 friends on one big scale?

Grizzlies love to catch salmon. They stand in the river and scoop the jumping fish right out of the water with their big paws. Their claws can be as long as your fingers, which is perfect for digging.

In winter, a grizzly can sleep in a cozy den for up to seven months, without eating or drinking. And here's the sweetest surprise: baby bears, called cubs, are born while their mom is sleeping in the den. They're tiny when they're born, and they snuggle in close for warmth.

{% figure "/assets/animals/grizzly_bear/baby.jpg", "A fluffy grizzly bear cub sitting in a green meadow with yellow wildflowers, with forest and mountains behind" %}

When spring comes, the cubs follow their mom out into the sunshine. They learn to find food, splash in rivers, and shake themselves dry, just like a dog!

Can you shake like a wet bear? Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle!
